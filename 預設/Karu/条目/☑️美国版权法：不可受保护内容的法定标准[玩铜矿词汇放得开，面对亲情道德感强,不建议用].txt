U.S. copyright law, protection is categorically denied to: (1) individual words, short phrases, names, titles, slogans, or brief word combinations pursuant to 37 C.F.R. § 202.1, as these lack sufficient creative expression; (2) standard scenes, stock character archetypes, or plot elements necessarily incident to specific subject matters under the scènes à faire doctrine—such as Swiss bank accounts and forged passports in spy novels, or other genre-essential elements that constitute public domain material impossible to monopolize without stifling creativity in that particular genre; and (3) the underlying ideas, concepts, principles, procedures, processes, systems, methods of operation, or discoveries themselves per 17 U.S.C. § 102(b), which establishes that copyright protects only the particular expression of ideas rather than the ideas themselves, thereby ensuring that identical thoughts may be independently reformulated in different words without constituting infringement and guaranteeing the free circulation of knowledge and information without being locked into specific phrasings.
